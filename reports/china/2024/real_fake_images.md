# Real or Fake Image Recognition Task — NOAI China 2024 Round 2

**Problem domain:** Binary image classification with architecture constraints  
**Evaluation metric:** If constraints are met, Score = (Network_Simplicity_Score + Accuracy) × 3/4, where Network_Simplicity_Score = 1 / (Num_Linear + Num_Conv + 1); otherwise score 0.

## Abridged statement

Train a small PyTorch CNN to distinguish real CIFAR-10 images from images generated by a diffusion model. The official statement uses labels 0 for real and 1 for fake, requires the class name `MyModel`, at least two `nn.Conv2d` and two `nn.MaxPool2d` layers, no more than two `nn.Linear` layers, and direct layer definitions rather than `nn.Sequential`. Every activation must be from `nn.ReLU`, `nn.Sigmoid`, `nn.Tanh`, `nn.ELU`, `nn.LeakyReLU`, or `nn.PReLU`. The published formula is `Score = (Network_Simplicity_Score + Accuracy) × 3/4`, with `Network_Simplicity_Score = 1 / (Num_Linear + Num_Conv + 1)`; violating the layer requirements gives zero. The official submission is `submission.zip` containing `submission_model.py` and `submission_dic.pth`.

## Dataset analysis and EDA

The accessible community mirror contains 10,000 PNGs: 5,000 in cifar/ (real, label 0) and 5,000 in uvit/ (generated, label 1). The loader checks that every image is 32×32 RGB. The classes are balanced, so chance accuracy is 50%. The mirror is not an authoritative Bohrium download, and the SOTA summary describes the organizer's hidden 2,000-image test set, which was not available locally. No image assets are stored in this repository.

## Experiments and selected method

[real_fake_images.py](../../../solutions/china/2024/real_fake_images.py) defines two direct convolution layers (3→24→48) for the selected global-mean model, each followed by ReLU and a max-pool. A spatial mean reduces the final map before one linear logits layer. This has two convolution layers and one linear layer, so its simplicity score is 1/(2+1+1) = 0.25. It is trained with BCEWithLogitsLoss and AdamW. The dense comparison uses 3→32→64 convolution widths and a 64-unit hidden linear layer.

| Candidate | Local holdout accuracy | Simplicity score | Implied task formula result | Provenance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Global-pooling head, 15 epochs | 0.7205 | 0.2500 | 0.7279 | Stratified 80/20 random holdout, seed 42, from the community mirror. (0.25 + 0.7205) × 0.75 = 0.727875, rounded to 0.7279. |
| Dense head, best of 10 printed epochs | 0.7400 | 0.2000 | 0.7050 | Same holdout; two linear layers. Best observed accuracy was at epoch 9. |
| Organizer leaderboard A/B accuracy | 1.0000 / 1.0000 | — | Not reported | Values stated in the third-party participant notebook; they are accuracy values, not a reproduced result here. |

The global-pooling candidate is selected by the task's composite formula on this split: its simplicity advantage outweighs the dense head's 1.95-point accuracy increase. The public notebook does not state its simplicity-adjusted leaderboard score. If its reported two-convolution/two-linear architecture passed inspection, the formula would imply 0.9000 at 1.000 accuracy; that is a calculation from the published formula, not a reported official score.

Train mode packages the model as `submission.zip`, with the standalone direct-layer `MyModel` in `submission_model.py` and raw state dict in `submission_dic.pth`. Its exported forward method returns sigmoid probabilities, as in the task starter model; training uses logits with `BCEWithLogitsLoss`. Example: `python solutions/china/2024/real_fake_images.py --mode train --data-dir /path/to/train --output submission.zip`. The package path was checked locally with synthetic weights; no organizer images were available for a full train-and-package run.

## Result, compute, and limits

The measured 15-epoch global-head validation run took about two minutes on CPU, including PNG loading and evaluation. **The 0.7279 value is a local proxy score computed from a random split of the community mirror, not an organizer leaderboard result.** The task notebook publishes the formula above and also mentions a possible accuracy-metric bug, without giving an alternate equation; this proxy uses the explicit published equation. The community repository metadata reports no license (license: null), and the SOTA page says the organizer license is unstated. Its images were used only from /tmp and are not included here. The actual hidden-test grader was inaccessible.

## Alternatives considered

- The two-linear-layer dense head increases accuracy on the local holdout but lowers the formula score due to its simplicity penalty.
- More convolution/linear layers can improve accuracy but lose simplicity points and may violate the caps.
- Pretrained models and external data are unnecessary for this constrained task and could violate the official rules.

## Progressive hints

1. Preserve the real/fake folder labels and confirm that the classes are balanced.
2. Use two convolution and max-pool stages to build local texture features.
3. Compare global average pooling with a small dense head using the full task formula, not accuracy alone.
4. Count the exact convolution and linear layers in MyModel before submission.

**One-line summary:** The simple 2-convolution, 1-linear CNN yields 0.7205 accuracy and a 0.7279 implied task score on a local mirrored-data holdout; official hidden-test accuracy remains unverified.
